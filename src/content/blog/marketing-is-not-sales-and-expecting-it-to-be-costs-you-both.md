They get spoken about as if they're the same job with two names. They aren't, and the gap between them is where a lot of good work quietly gets killed. Here's how we think about it.

## Where the confusion starts

Both of them end in money, so they get judged by the same number, usually in the same meeting. Somebody asks what marketing brought in this month, and whatever the answer is, it is the wrong answer, because that is not the question marketing is built to answer.

Sales closes the person who is already in front of you. Marketing decides how many people are in front of you, what they already believe about you when they arrive, and whether they arrive warm or skeptical or comparing you against three cheaper options.

Those are different jobs on different clocks. A good salesperson can tell you what happened this week. Good marketing is frequently the reason next quarter looks the way it does, and it will almost never be able to hand you a clean receipt for that.

## What it looks like when you judge one by the other

We see the same three things happen, over and over.

A campaign gets killed at week three because it has not produced revenue yet, when nobody has been through a full consideration cycle. For most of our clients that cycle is measured in weeks or months, not days, and the campaign was pulled before a single person got to the end of it.

Brand work gets cut first, because it is the hardest to attribute. And the cut does not hurt for about two quarters, which is exactly what makes it dangerous. By the time it shows up, it shows up everywhere at once: leads cost more, close slower, and negotiate harder, because nobody has heard of you.

And last click gets treated as truth. So the branded search ad, which was the very last step of a journey that started somewhere else entirely, gets all the credit, and the thing that actually created the demand gets defunded for underperforming.

## Sometimes it is not a marketing problem at all

This is the uncomfortable half, and we would rather say it early than discover it in month four.

We have watched campaigns deliver genuinely good inquiries into a business that could not catch them. The form went to an inbox nobody owned. The response came five days later. The follow-up was one email, and when it went unanswered, that was the end of it. Then marketing gets reviewed for poor lead quality.

So before we spend your money, we want to see what happens after the lead arrives. Who receives it, how fast, what they say, and how many times they try. If that part is not in place, more traffic will not help you. It will just cost you more to lose the same people.

This works in the other direction too. If your sales team is closing well but there is nobody new to talk to, that is not a sales problem. That is the marketing bill coming due.

## What marketing is actually accountable for

None of this is a case for work you cannot measure. We are not asking anyone to take brand on faith, and we do not use the long game as a place to hide.

Marketing should be answerable for whether the right people know you exist, whether they understand what you do within a few seconds of finding you, whether they arrive already trusting you a little, and whether the volume and quality of what reaches your sales process is going up. Those are all trackable. We track them. They are simply not the same as revenue closed this month, and pretending otherwise is how businesses end up with a pipeline that looks fine right up until it does not.

The honest version is this. Marketing makes sales easier, cheaper, and more likely. It does not replace it, and it cannot rescue a process that drops people.

## So, not the same job. The same goal.

The brands that get this right stop asking marketing to behave like sales, and start asking a better question: is it getting easier to win the business we want?

Easier to get the meeting. Easier to explain what you do. Less time spent justifying your pricing, because the reputation walked into the room first.

When that is happening, marketing is working, whether or not this month's number says so. When it is not, more sales pressure will not fix it, and we would rather tell you that on the first call than the fourth invoice.
