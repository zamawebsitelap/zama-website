Almost nobody is failing at social media because they aren't trying. They're posting. They're showing up. Here's what I actually see going wrong, and what we do differently.

## A calendar is not a strategy

Here is the thing I see most often, and it is almost always the root of the other four. A brand shows me its content calendar, and it is full. Thirty squares, every one of them spoken for. And when I ask why this post and not a different one, the answer is some version of we had the photo.

A calendar tells you when you are posting. It does not tell you why. When it is the only planning document in the room, content gets chosen by what is easy to produce that week rather than by what the brand actually needs to prove. So the feed stays busy and stays forgettable. Nothing builds on anything.

So we start with three or four themes the brand has real permission to own, tied to what it sells and to the thing buyers quietly hesitate over. Then the calendar fills itself. And when a trend shows up that does not belong to any of those themes, you get to skip it without feeling like you are missing out, which is a surprisingly good feeling.

## Reach is the number everyone reports and nobody acts on

Impressions are the easiest thing to put in a deck and the hardest thing to do anything with. I have seen a post reach eighty thousand people and produce nothing. I have seen a quiet one reach four hundred of exactly the right people and fill a consultation calendar for a month.

I am not against reach. I am against reach standing in for evidence when nobody has checked what happened next.

So before we publish anything, we make the measurement real. Tags on every link. Conversion tracking confirmed to be firing, not assumed to be firing. A plain line of sight from post to visit to inquiry. It is unglamorous setup work and most brands skip it, and then we spend a quarter arguing about numbers that were never going to answer the question.

## Five platforms at sixty percent

A small team running five active channels is usually doing five things adequately and nothing well. And the Pinterest account nobody has touched since spring is not neutral. It is a signal, and not the one you want, to anyone who finds it.

Platforms are not obligations. Each one is a real cost in production time and attention, and choosing one means choosing against something else.

We would rather run two channels properly than five adequately, every single time. Pick where your buyer actually decides, not where your competitor happens to be loudest. Claim the rest, brand them, point them back to the site, and leave them alone until you have the capacity to do them properly. There is no shame in a channel you are not running yet. There is real cost in one you are running badly.

## Somewhere along the way, the brand started sounding like the algorithm

This one is quieter than the others and it does the most damage over time. A brand spends years building a voice that is specific and considered, and then hands the account over to whatever the platform is rewarding this month. The audio is trending, so we use the audio. The format is sarcastic, so suddenly we are sarcastic.

Engagement might even tick up. Recognition goes down, because nothing in the feed is recognizably yours anymore.

Trends are a format, not a personality. The test we use is simple and a little blunt: cover the logo. If someone who knows your brand would still know it was you, it is on voice. If the post could belong to any of two hundred other accounts, we write it again.

## The comment section is not cleanup

Most brands treat comments and DMs as a chore for whoever has a free ten minutes. But that is where the actual buying conversation is happening. Someone asking about pricing or availability in a reply is closer to saying yes than anyone who simply scrolled past the post.

Leaving that sitting for four days, or answering with a bare link, is the most direct leak I know of in this channel.

So we treat replies the way we treat content. Someone owns it. There is a response window. There is language ready for the questions that repeat, so nobody is writing from scratch at 9pm. And there is a clear path to a human the moment something has heat on it.

## So, less posting. More deciding.

None of this needs a bigger budget. It needs you to decide what the brand stands for, choose fewer places to say it, measure the handful of things that actually matter, and treat the people replying to you like customers instead of noise.

That is usually the whole gap between a brand that posts and a brand that is known.

If yours feels busy but somehow flat, I would bet it is one of these five. All of them are fixable inside a quarter, and I would love to show you what that looks like in practice.
